# Slide Brief — [Talk title]

> **This document is a handoff.** It specifies **what each slide must communicate** and
> nothing about how it should look. It is written to be handed, whole and unedited, to a
> deck-building skill (`pptx`, `ppt-master`), a designer, or the speaker.

---

## To whoever builds this deck

**Your job:** turn each row below into one slide that lands its stated point.
**Not your job:** deciding what the points are. That's settled — it came out of the talk's
structure, and changing it breaks the talk.

**Free to decide:** layout, typography, palette, template, imagery style, grid, motion,
chart styling — every visual choice. No design direction is given here on purpose.

**Must respect:**

1. **One point per slide.** The "The point" column is that slide's entire job. If a slide
   can't carry it alone, split it — never merge two rows.
2. **Do not put the speaker's words on the slide.** Narration plus identical on-screen text
   measurably *reduces* comprehension (the redundancy effect). The "Speaker says" column is
   context so you know what the slide sits under — it is not slide copy.
3. **Do not add slides.** A deck longer than this brief means the speaker is now clicking
   during sentences they planned to say to a face.
4. **Do not turn a point into a bullet list.** If a row says "one number," it is one number.
5. **Headlines stay assertions.** Where a headline is given, keep its claim. You may rewrite
   it for length or tone; you may not turn "Churn is concentrated in month two" back into
   "Q3 Churn."
6. **Honor the `[BLANK]` rows.** A deliberately black screen is a designed moment, not a
   gap to fill.

## Does this talk need slides?

**The test:** does any beat require the audience to *see* something — an image, the shape of
data, a live demo, a face, a physical comparison?

- [ ] **No beat does** → no slides, or one title card. *A legitimate and sometimes correct
      answer. Say so plainly rather than building a deck out of obligation.*
- [ ] **A few beats do** → slide only those; blank the screen for the rest

**Column definitions**

- **Hold** — how long this slide stays up. Under ~20 seconds means it should probably merge
  with its neighbor; rapid clicking fragments attention and reads as nervousness.
- **The point** — one assertion. If you cannot write this cell, **delete the row**: a slide
  with no point is a slide with no reason to exist.
- **Must be visible** — the content that has to be on screen, named concretely enough to
  build without asking. *"The actual 14-page onboarding form, photographed flat"* — not
  *"an image about paperwork."* State the type: photo / chart / one number / quote / demo /
  diagram / blank.
- **Evidence / source** — where the data or asset comes from, or `[ASSET NEEDED]`.
- **Speaker says** — the gist of the narration, so the builder understands the moment.
  **Never slide copy.**

## Leave-behind

*A deck sparse enough to present is too sparse to read alone; a deck dense enough to read
alone is too dense to present. Decide explicitly rather than building a hybrid that fails
at both.*

- [ ] Live deck only
- [ ] Live deck + a separate written memo *(recommended when the read-alone version matters)*
- [ ] Live deck + appendix, clearly separated
- [ ] Density lives in speaker notes; export the notes view

## Builder checklist

- [ ] Every row's "The point" cell is filled — no orphan slides
- [ ] No slide reproduces the speaker's sentences
- [ ] Every headline is an assertion
- [ ] Every slide passes the glance test: 3 seconds to comprehend
- [ ] No slide held under ~20 seconds
- [ ] `[BLANK]` moments preserved
- [ ] Charts: one message each, title states the message, the point is annotated
- [ ] Readable from the back of the room
