## Rehearsal ladder

| # | Pass | Where | Job | Done | Runtime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read | Sitting, quiet | Find broken sentences | ☐ | |
| 2 | Stand | Standing, full volume | Find breath problems, dead beats | ☐ | |
| 3 | **Beats** | Standing, notes only | **Prove the structure holds without the script** | ☐ | |
| 4 | Full | With slides + clicker, timed | The real run | ☐ | |
| 5 | Hostile | With one interrupter | Q&A, recovering after interruption | ☐ | |

**Pass 3 is the one people skip and the one that matters.** Where you get lost is a
structural flaw, not a memory flaw — the audience would have gotten lost there too. Go fix
the beat sheet; don't memorize harder.

## Room and tech

*Thirty minutes before.*

- [ ] Walk the room; stand at the back and read your smallest slide
- [ ] Clicker tested, fresh batteries; know the blank-screen key (`B`)
- [ ] Audio tested (the most common live failure)
- [ ] Aspect ratio confirmed on the actual projector
- [ ] Deck in two places (laptop + USB or cloud)
- [ ] PDF fallback exported
- [ ] Know where you'll stand, and where the clock is
- [ ] Water, opened, within reach
- [ ] Phone off — not silent, off
- [ ] Know who introduces you and what they'll say

**If virtual, also:**

- [ ] Camera at eye level
- [ ] Light from in front, not behind
- [ ] Marked which lines to deliver looking at the lens
- [ ] Interaction planned every few minutes
- [ ] Names of specific people to address directly

## Contingencies

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| **You blank** | Stop. Look at notes. Say the last thing you're sure of aloud. The silence is shorter than it feels |
| **Tech dies** | Continue without it. You know the structure — that's why you memorized beats, not slides |
| **Losing the room** | Change mode: a question, a move, a story. Don't just talk louder |
| **Running long** | Go to pre-marked cuts. Deliver the close in full |
| **Hostile question** | Agree with what's true, then address the rest. The room is grading composure |
| **Cut short live** | Which two beats + the close? → Beats ___ and ___ |

---

## Day-of

- [ ] Arrive early; occupy the space
- [ ] Talk to three people who'll be in the audience
- [ ] Four in, six out — one minute of breathing before you're introduced
- [ ] Reappraise: "I'm excited," not "calm down"
- [ ] Deliberately slow the first minute
- [ ] Before the first sentence: stand still, look at them, wait two seconds
- [ ] After the last sentence: stop. Don't fill the silence
